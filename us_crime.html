US Crime

In this project, I analyzed a dataset of over 1 million crime records from 2020 to 2025 using Python, Pandas, and Matplotlib. I explored crime trends, discovering a significant drop in 2024, and identified Central as the area with the highest crime rates. I also analyzed crime locations, types, and weapon usage, revealing streets and residential areas as the most common crime spots, with firearms and knives being the most frequently used weapons. This project highlights my skills in data cleaning and visualization.

Crime Trend Over Years

This line chart visualizes the trend of reported crimes from 2020 to 2025, using the datetime OCC data. The x-axis represents the years, and the y-axis shows the number of crimes reported.

  • 2020: The crime rate starts at around 200,000.
  • 2021: There is a slight increase in crime by about 1,000 incidents.
  • 2022: The crime rate continues to rise by approximately 2,000 incidents.
  • 2023: The crime trend slightly decreases, dropping by around 800-1,000 incidents.
  • 2024: A significant drop occurs, with crime levels falling from about 200,000 to 100,000.
  • 2025: The trend further decreases to nearly zero, although this could be due to the early stage of the year.

Key Insights:

  • The steady increase in crime from 2020 to 2022 indicates a growing trend in criminal activity.
  • In 2023, a slight decline might suggest some changes in crime patterns, possibly due to law enforcement efforts or social changes.
  • The sharp drop in 2024 could indicate significant interventions, such as improved policing, changes in law enforcement, or broader societal shifts that reduced crime.
  • 2025 seems to show a near-zero crime rate, though the early months of the year could explain this, requiring further data to confirm the trend.

Common Crime Locations

Key Insights:

  • Streets have the highest crime count by a huge margin, making them the most common crime locations.
  • Single-family dwellings and multi-unit dwellings (apartments, duplexes, etc.) also have high crime rates, suggesting that residential areas experience significant criminal activity.
  • Parking lots, sidewalks, and vehicles (passenger/truck) are also common crime locations.
  • Gas stations, yards, clothing stores, and parks/playgrounds have the lowest crime rates in the dataset.

Possible Interpretations:

  • Crimes in streets might include theft, assaults, and other street-level offenses.
  • High crime in residential areas could indicate break-ins, burglaries, or domestic incidents.
  • Parking lots and underground parking being crime-prone suggests vehicle-related crimes like theft and vandalism.
